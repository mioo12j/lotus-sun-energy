Rooftop vs Ground-Mount Solar: Which Is Right for Your Site?

Use the roof you already have, or build on land? A practical comparison of rooftop and ground-mount solar: cost, space, structure, safety, foundations, approvals and maintenance.

By the Lotus Sun Energy team · Updated 11 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways
  • Rooftop uses space you already have and avoids land, fencing and piling costs
  • Ground-mount gives freedom to size, orient and maintain the plant, and avoids old or weak roofs
  • Industrial sheet roofs need a structural check, purlin-mounted structures and skylight safety mesh
  • Ground-mount needs a soil test, pile foundations sized to it, fencing and drainage
  • Many sites combine both: rooftop on the sound sheds, ground-mount on spare land

Rooftop: what makes a good roof

  • Strength: a structural engineer confirms the roof and purlins can carry the added load and wind uplift.
  • Remaining life: re-roof first if the sheets have only a few years left; removing a plant to re-roof is costly.
  • Shade-free area: watch for parapets, tanks, chimneys and neighbouring buildings.
  • Safety for life: skylight safety mesh over translucent sheets, lifelines and walkways for cleaning crews.

Ground-mount: what makes a good site

  • Soil test first: it decides pile depth and diameter, and whether augered or driven piles suit.
  • Drainage and flooding: low-lying land needs raised structures and drains.
  • Evacuation: the distance to your meter or the grid connection point.
  • Security: chain-link fencing, gates and lighting.
  • Land rules: check land use and any state limits on solar on agricultural land before you build.

When to combine both

Plenty of sites use both: rooftop on sound sheds near the main load, ground-mount on spare land for the rest. The two can feed the same distribution board or separate meters, depending on your connection and state rules.

Frequently asked questions

Is rooftop solar cheaper than ground-mount?
Usually per kWp, because there is no land, fencing or piling. Ground-mount can be competitive at larger scale and gives more freedom in size and orientation.
How much land does a 1 MW ground-mount solar plant need?
Roughly 4 to 5 acres for a fixed-tilt plant, depending on module efficiency, row spacing and the shape of the land.
Can any factory roof take solar panels?
Not always. A structural check of the sheets, purlins and trusses is essential, and old roofs should be replaced before installation.
Why do sheet roofs need skylight mesh?
Translucent skylight sheets cannot take a person's weight. Safety mesh under or over them protects installers and cleaning crews.
