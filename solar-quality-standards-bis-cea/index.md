# Solar Quality and Safety Norms in India (2026): BIS, the Quality Control Order, CEA and CEIG

What makes a solar plant compliant in 2026: the BIS Quality Control Order for modules, inverters and batteries, the standards each component should meet, the Electrical Inspector's approval, and site safety.

**Key takeaways**

- The revised **Solar Systems, Devices and Components Goods Order, 2025** (notified 27 January 2025) requires BIS certification for modules, solar inverters and storage batteries
- Large inverters (above 200 kW) were given extra time to certify, reportedly to the end of 2026
- Modules must also meet ALMM where it applies (see our ALMM guide)
- Plants above the voltage and capacity thresholds need the **Electrical Inspector's (CEIG) approval** before energisation under the CEA safety regulations
- Earthing, lightning protection, surge protection and documented commissioning tests are what keep a plant safe for 25 years

## Three layers of compliance

- **Product certification:** each component meets its Indian Standard and carries the BIS mark (and, for modules, ALMM where applicable).
- **Design and installation:** the plant is designed and built to the electrical safety regulations and good practice.
- **Inspection and approval:** the DISCOM and, where required, the Electrical Inspector approve the plant before it is energised.

## The BIS Quality Control Order

MNRE notified the revised **Solar Systems, Devices and Components Goods (Requirement for Compulsory Registration) Order, 2025** in the Gazette on **27 January 2025**, effective after a 180-day transition. It covers **solar PV modules, inverters for solar PV applications and storage batteries**. These must conform to the notified Indian Standards and carry the BIS Standard Mark under a BIS licence; products made only for export are exempt.

Inverters above 200 kW were allowed extra time. MNRE extended the self-certification window for them, reportedly to **31 December 2026**, during which makers need valid IEC certificates matching the Indian Standards.

## The standards each component should meet

Common standards for solar plant components

| Component | Standards to ask for | What they cover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crystalline PV modules | IS 14286 / IEC 61215; IS/IEC 61730 | Design qualification and type approval; safety |
| Grid-tied inverters | IS 16221 (Parts 1 and 2) / IEC 62109; IS 16169 | Safety of power converters; islanding-prevention test |
| DC cables | IS 17293 / EN 50618 type solar cables | UV-resistant, double-insulated cables for PV |
| AC power cables | IS 7098 (XLPE) | Armoured LT cables to the distribution board |
| Earthing | IS 3043 | Code of practice for earthing |
| Lightning protection | IS/IEC 62305 | Lightning protection of structures |
| Commissioning and documentation | IEC 62446-1 | System documentation, commissioning tests and inspection |
| Storage batteries | Notified IS for the battery type | Covered by the 2025 Quality Control Order |

**Standards are revised**

BIS revises standards and adds parts over time, and sources differ on which part covers what. Ask the supplier for the BIS licence and test reports for the exact model supplied, and keep them in the project file.

## CEA safety regulations and CEIG approval

The Central Electricity Authority's **safety and electric supply regulations** set the rules for electrical installations, including clearances, earthing, protection and inspection. Installations above the specified voltage, and in many states above a set capacity, must be inspected and approved by the **Electrical Inspector** (the Chief Electrical Inspector to Government, CEIG, in many states) before they are energised.

- **Drawing approval**Submit single-line diagram, layout and equipment details for approval where the state requires it.
- **Build to the approved design**Install with the specified protection, earthing and clearances.
- **Test**Insulation resistance, earth resistance, protection settings and relay tests, recorded on the prescribed formats.
- **Inspection**The Electrical Inspector inspects the installation and the test records.
- **Approval to energise**With approval, the DISCOM completes metering and synchronisation.

## Safety on site

- Permit-to-work and electrical isolation (lock-out, tag-out) before any work on live DC or AC circuits
- Work-at-height controls on roofs: lifelines, harnesses, edge protection and skylight safety mesh
- PPE suited to the task: helmets, insulated gloves, safety shoes, eye protection
- Daily toolbox talks and supervision by trained engineers
- A safety management system certified to ISO 45001

## Frequently asked questions

Do solar inverters need BIS certification?

Yes. Under the 2025 Quality Control Order, inverters for solar PV applications must carry the BIS Standard Mark. Inverters above 200 kW were given extra time, reportedly to the end of 2026.

Which standards should solar modules meet?

IS 14286 / IEC 61215 for design qualification and IS/IEC 61730 for safety, and ALMM listing where the project falls under the ALMM order.

What is CEIG approval for solar?

Approval by the Electrical Inspector that the installation meets the CEA safety regulations, required before energising plants above the specified voltage or, in many states, above a set capacity.

What tests should be done at commissioning?

Insulation resistance, polarity, string open-circuit voltage and short-circuit current, earth resistance, protection settings and functional checks, documented to IEC 62446-1.
