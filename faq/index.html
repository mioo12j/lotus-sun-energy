Cost and savings

How much does rooftop solar cost for a home in India?
As a guide, a home rooftop system costs roughly Rs 55,000 to Rs 65,000 per kW before subsidy, depending on the roof, the structure height and the components. A typical 3 kW home system therefore costs about Rs 1.7 to 2 lakh, and the PM Surya Ghar subsidy brings Rs 78,000 of that back. Use our savings calculator for your own numbers; the exact price comes after a free site visit.
How much will I save on my electricity bill?
Most homes that size the system to their usage cut their bill by 70 to 90%. Each kW of panels makes roughly 4 units a day on average in most of India (about 1,400 to 1,500 units a year), so at Rs 7 to 8 a unit a 3 kW system saves around Rs 2,500 to 3,000 a month.
How long does solar take to pay for itself?
For a home with the subsidy, typically 3 to 5 years. Businesses without the subsidy usually see 3 to 6 years, depending on the tariff and how much of the power is used during the day. After that the system keeps generating for 25 years or more.
What size of system do I need?
A quick rule: bills around Rs 1,000 to 2,500 a month suit 1 to 2 kW, Rs 2,500 to 4,500 suit about 3 kW, and bills above Rs 5,000 need 5 kW or more. We confirm the size from your bills, sanctioned load and roof during the site visit.

Subsidy and finance

What government subsidy can I get for home solar?
Under PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana the central subsidy is Rs 30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and Rs 18,000 for the 3rd kW: Rs 30,000 for 1 kW, Rs 60,000 for 2 kW and Rs 78,000 for 3 kW or more. Housing societies get Rs 18,000 per kW for common facilities. Some states add their own top-up.
Who is eligible for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?
A household with its own electricity connection, a suitable roof it owns, and no earlier solar subsidy on the same connection. The system must be grid-connected with net metering and use Indian-made (DCR) modules, installed by a vendor registered on the portal.
Do you handle the subsidy paperwork?
Yes. We register you on the national portal, file the DISCOM feasibility and net-metering application, arrange the inspection after installation and follow the claim until the subsidy reaches your bank account.
Can I pay in EMIs?
Yes. Banks offer solar loans, and for home systems up to 3 kW the scheme provides collateral-free loans at around 7% interest. In many cases the EMI is close to the bill it replaces, so you save from the first month. We guide you through the paperwork.
Do businesses get any benefit?
Businesses do not get the PM Surya Ghar subsidy, but a business that owns its plant can usually claim accelerated depreciation, and GST on solar modules is 5%. Ask your chartered accountant to confirm.

Your roof and site

Is my roof suitable for solar?
Most are. You need roughly 80 to 100 sq ft of shade-free roof per kW, facing south, east or west, without tall trees or buildings shading it between 9 AM and 4 PM. RCC and metal-sheet roofs both work; we check the strength and shade on the site visit.
Will the panels damage my roof or cause leaks?
No. On RCC roofs we use raised structures on concrete or chemically anchored bases with waterproofing at each fixing; on metal sheets we use clamps or sealed fixings matched to the sheet profile. The structure is designed for the wind speed of your area.
I live in a rented house or flat. Can I still go solar?
The subsidy needs a roof the applicant owns. In an apartment, the housing society can install solar for common areas (lifts, pumps, lighting) and claim the society subsidy. Talk to us and we will suggest what is possible.
Do you install ground-mount solar on open land?
Yes. For farms, factories and institutions with spare land we design and build ground-mount plants, including soil checks, pile foundations, structures, fencing and the grid connection. See ground-mount solar.

Installation and approvals

How long does installation take?
For a typical home, the physical installation takes 2 to 4 days. The whole process, including portal registration, DISCOM feasibility, net meter and inspection, usually takes a few weeks, depending on your DISCOM.
What is net metering?
A two-way meter records the power you draw from the grid and the surplus solar power you send to it. You pay only for the net units, and summer surplus can offset monsoon months within the settlement period. See our net metering guide.
Does solar work during a power cut?
A standard grid-connected (on-grid) system shuts down during a grid outage for the safety of line workers. If you want power during cuts, choose a hybrid system with batteries; we will explain the cost difference honestly.
Which panels and inverters do you use?
We do not manufacture equipment. We specify BIS-certified, ALMM-listed modules (Indian-made DCR modules where the subsidy needs them) and reliable on-grid or hybrid inverters with monitoring, with the makes and warranties written into your quote.

Maintenance and performance

Does solar work in the monsoon and in winter?
Yes, at lower output. Panels generate on cloudy days, just less. Over a year the sunny months more than make up for it, and net metering lets you bank the surplus.
How much maintenance does solar need?
Very little: rinse the panels with plain water every 2 to 4 weeks (more often in dusty areas), keep shade-causing branches trimmed, and get a yearly check of the wiring, earthing and inverter. We offer maintenance plans for larger systems.
How long do solar panels last?
Quality modules come with a 25 to 30 year performance warranty and keep producing well beyond that, losing only a small percentage of output each year. Inverters typically last 10 to 15 years.
