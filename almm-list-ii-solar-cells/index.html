ALMM List-I and List-II Explained: The Made-in-India Rule for Solar Modules and Cells

MNRE's Approved List of Models and Manufacturers now covers solar cells as well as modules. What List-I and List-II mean, which projects they bind, the exemptions, and how to stay compliant.

By the Lotus Sun Energy team · Updated 11 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways
  • ALMM List-I covers solar modules; List-II, for solar cells, applies from 1 June 2026
  • Covered projects must use List-I modules that are themselves made with List-II cells
  • Projects whose bid deadline fell on or before the amendment are exempt from the cell requirement; net-metering and open-access projects commissioned before 1 June 2026 were exempted too
  • MNRE has signalled a third list, for wafers, later in the decade (reported for June 2028)
  • Ask for the ALMM listing, the model number and the cell source in writing before you order modules

What ALMM is and why it exists

The Approved List of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM) is a register kept by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy. Only the module models and manufacturers on it may be used in the projects the ALMM Order covers. MNRE issued the order in 2019 to make sure that solar plants built with public support or for public utilities use reliable equipment from verified factories, and to build up manufacturing in India.

Until 2026 the order had one list, List-I for solar PV modules. MNRE then amended the ALMM Order 2019 to add List-II for solar PV cells, with effect from 1 June 2026.

In practice List-II means that a covered project needs modules whose cells were also made in an ALMM-listed Indian factory. A module assembled in India from imported cells stays on List-I but no longer satisfies a project that needs List-II cells.

Which projects must comply

The order applies to projects set out in it, chiefly:

  • Government and government-assisted projects, including those under central schemes
  • Projects selling power to the government or to DISCOMs, such as plants built under central or state tenders
  • Projects under schemes that require it, such as PM-KUSUM and PM Surya Ghar (which already insist on domestic-content modules)
  • Net-metering and open-access projects, which MNRE has brought within the order's scope, subject to the exemptions below

The exemptions

  • Projects whose bid submission deadline was on or before the date of the List-II amendment are excused from the cell requirement, though they still need List-I modules.
  • Net-metering and open-access projects commissioned before 1 June 2026 were exempt from sourcing List-II cells.
Purely private captive plants

Whether a privately funded captive plant must use ALMM modules depends on how it connects and sells power, and on your state's rules. Many clients choose ALMM equipment anyway for quality and future flexibility. Check the position for your project before ordering.

What it means for price and supply

Indian cell capacity is growing quickly but is smaller than module capacity, so modules with domestic cells (often called DCR modules) carry a premium over modules with imported cells. Lead times can also be longer in peak months.

Plan for it: fix the module specification at the design stage, get written confirmation of the ALMM listing and cell source from the supplier, and lock delivery dates into the project schedule.

How to check compliance before you order

  1. Find the listDownload the current List-I and List-II from MNRE's ALMM pages; the lists are updated regularly.
  2. Match the modelCheck the exact module model number, wattage and factory address against List-I.
  3. Trace the cellsAsk the module maker for the cell manufacturer and factory, and check it against List-II.
  4. Keep the paper trailKeep invoices, the ALMM extract and the maker's declaration. DISCOMs, scheme portals and inspectors can ask for them.
  5. Inspect on deliveryMatch nameplates and serial numbers on site with the documents before installation starts.

Lotus Sun Energy does not manufacture modules or inverters. We specify ALMM-listed (and, where the subsidy needs it, DCR) makes, and we check and record what arrives on site, so the documents are ready when the DISCOM or inspector asks.

Frequently asked questions

What is ALMM List-II?
The list of approved solar cell manufacturers, added to MNRE's ALMM Order 2019. From 1 June 2026 covered projects must use List-I modules that are made with List-II cells.
Is ALMM mandatory for rooftop solar?
It is mandatory for projects covered by the order, including scheme-backed rooftop solar such as PM Surya Ghar. For other rooftop plants it depends on the connection and the state rules; many owners choose ALMM equipment anyway.
What are DCR modules?
Domestic Content Requirement modules: modules made with Indian cells. Schemes such as PM Surya Ghar and PM-KUSUM require them.
Will there be an ALMM list for wafers?
MNRE has signalled a List-III for wafers, reported for June 2028. It is not yet in force; watch for the notification.
